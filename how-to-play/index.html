How to play Sudoku

Sudoku is a logic puzzle played on a 9×9 grid. You do not need maths: it is all about spotting where numbers can and cannot go. This guide takes you from the rules to the first techniques that solve most easy and medium puzzles.

The rules

The grid has 81 cells, split into nine 3×3 boxes. Some cells already contain numbers (the "givens"). Fill the rest so that:

  • every row contains the digits 1 to 9 exactly once,
  • every column contains the digits 1 to 9 exactly once,
  • every 3×3 box contains the digits 1 to 9 exactly once.

A well-made puzzle has exactly one solution and can always be solved with logic. In Sudoku Grid every puzzle is checked to have a single solution, so you never need to guess.

How to start

Begin where there is the most information: rows, columns and boxes that are already almost full. Ask yourself, "which number is missing here?" and look for the cell where it can go. Also look at digits that appear many times on the board (for example, six or seven 5s): they are the easiest to finish.

Technique 1: scanning

Pick a digit, say 7. Find every 7 on the board. Each one rules out the whole row, column and box it sits in. Look at one box that has no 7 yet: if the rows and columns of other 7s block all but one cell, the 7 must go in that cell. Repeat for every digit; each pass usually reveals a few new numbers.

Technique 2: single candidates

Naked single

A cell where only one digit is possible, because the other eight already appear in its row, column or box. Place that digit.

Hidden single

A digit that can only go in one cell of a row, column or box, even if that cell could hold other digits too. Look at a row and ask, "where can the 4 go?" If there is only one place, that is the answer.

Pencil notes

When the easy numbers run out, write small candidate numbers in the cells (pencil notes). They let you see patterns that are hard to keep in your head. Whenever you place a number, remove it from the notes of every cell in its row, column and box. In Sudoku Grid this clean-up is automatic, and the board highlights matching numbers to help you.

Technique 3: naked pairs

If two cells in the same row, column or box both have exactly the same two candidates (say 2 and 6), then those two digits must occupy those two cells. You can safely remove 2 and 6 from the notes of every other cell in that row, column or box. This often unlocks a stuck puzzle.

Common mistakes

  • Guessing. If you are stuck, look for another technique instead. Guessing usually leads to a long chain of errors.
  • Forgetting the boxes. Beginners check rows and columns but forget the 3×3 box.
  • Not updating notes. Out-of-date notes create false candidates.
  • Rushing. A single wrong digit can make the rest impossible. Take your time.

Tips to improve

  • Start with Easy and move up when you can finish without hints.
  • Use hints to learn: after a hint, ask yourself why that digit goes there.
  • Play a little every day; pattern recognition improves quickly with practice.
  • When stuck, take a short break. A fresh look often shows the next move.
Try it now. Click or tap a cell, then a number. On a keyboard, use 1–9, N for notes and Z to undo.
